The story

From NYC subway platforms to the NBA All-Star stage

Elec Simon started out busking on the streets of New York City, turning buckets, pipes and whatever else he could find into a full drum kit and a crowd of strangers into an audience.

That led to a decade touring the world with STOMP, the percussion phenomenon — and then to the hardwood, performing with the Cleveland Cavaliers’ Q-Stix, including NBA 2016 All-Star Weekend in Toronto.

Today he’s Orlando-based and on the road year-round as an MC and percussionist, hosting corporate conferences, weddings and festivals, and bringing bucket-drumming anti-bullying assemblies into schools across the country.

Elec’s first instrument was whatever was in front of him. Growing up, he drummed on lunch tables, on cardboard, on five-gallon buckets — and by the time he was performing on the streets of New York, he had turned that instinct into a craft. Busking taught him the skill that still defines his work: how to stop a moving crowd and hold it.

Joining STOMP took that skill worldwide. Across roughly a decade with the production, he performed in theaters across the United States and internationally, learning the discipline of a tightly cued show — timing, transitions, and the physical storytelling of rhythm without a single spoken line.

Back in Ohio, Elec became a fixture of the Cleveland Cavaliers’ game-night experience as part of the Q-Stix, the team’s bucket-drumming crew, performing for sold-out arenas and on the league’s biggest stages — including NBA All-Star Weekend in Toronto in 2016.

Alongside the touring, Elec built a school program he considers his most important work: interactive anti-bullying assemblies where students come on stage, pick up sticks, and play along. The message is simple and it lands because the kids are part of it — tolerance, love, and understanding, delivered at volume.

Now based in Orlando, Elec works year-round as an event MC and percussion entertainer for corporate conferences, brand activations, weddings, festivals and schools, with clients including Zillow, Lowe’s, Synchrony Bank and the Summit of Greatness. Whatever the room, the job is the same as it was on those subway platforms: no dead spots, just energy.
